Two narrow agents, not one: scoping AI help around a WhatsApp cohort's retention loop

Teams that run several paid cohorts are starting to put AI assistants next to their group tooling. The temptation is a single agent that can read everything and post anywhere. For a retention loop that is the wrong shape. The loop has a read side (who went quiet) and a write side (what gets said to the group), and they deserve separate scopes.

This note applies least privilege to the routine described in 9bot's write up on keeping student communities active. 9bot is a paid subscription with a seven day evaluation period and is independent of Meta; nothing here implies a WhatsApp endorsement.

The loop being assisted

Weekly, the team reads members inactive for seven days in 9bot Reports, crosses them with course progress, posts an easy milestone and a poll in the group, and hands the remaining silent students to a tutor for a private message. Near the end, Time based Access Control sends a notice before removal.

Scope split

Agent May read May write Must never
Analyst Reports (active, 7 day inactive, exits), course progress export A draft summary for the team Post in any group, contact a student
Drafter Milestone calendar, command registry Draft milestone and poll texts for human approval Read member lists, change access periods
Human tutor Everything above Private messages, final approval of drafts Delegate private outreach to an agent

The private message stays with a person for two reasons. It is the step where judgment matters most, and 9bot itself does not send private messages to inactive students; it covers group messages, commands, moderation, reports and timed access.

A scope declaration

Illustrative only. Adapt it to whatever agent framework or MCP client you use; it does not describe a 9bot configuration file.

{
  "agents": {
    "retention-analyst": {
      "read": ["reports.inactive_7d", "reports.active", "reports.exits", "lms.progress_export"],
      "write": ["drafts/weekly-summary.md"],
      "deny": ["group.post", "member.contact", "access.update"]
    },
    "milestone-drafter": {
      "read": ["calendar.milestones", "commands.registry"],
      "write": ["drafts/milestones/*.md"],
      "deny": ["reports.*", "member.*", "access.*"],
      "requires_human_approval": true
    }
  }
}

Why the split helps

  • A compromised or confused drafter cannot see who is struggling, so it cannot single anyone out in a public post.
  • The analyst cannot post, so a bad summary stays a bad document instead of becoming a message to fifty paying customers.
  • Member data stays in fewer places. The data protection paper in the series at 9-bot.github.io stresses that group members already see each other's numbers; agents should not widen that surface.

Limits

The evidence behind the article is a survey published in 2024 in Frontiers in Education, with 309 respondents about to graduate, in groups their institution ran; it ties reciprocity and satisfaction to a stated wish to keep going. Agents do not change that evidence. They only make the weekly routine cheaper to run. Measure completion on the platform before and after.
